
1. What question does the study test?

• The Answer: The study tests a clinical methodology question: How robustly do alternative single-patient analytical estimators (Paired Comparisons, Time-Series Regression, and Bayesian Models) recover a true individual therapeutic response (\(\delta_i = 0.50\text{ mmHg}\)) out of non-stationary remote patient monitoring streams contaminated by hardware data gaps, motion artifacts, and prolonged physiological drug carryover? It explicitly tests the exact point where standard linear models fail compared to Bayesian structures under parameterized data-quality stress.

2. What is simulated, and what is empirically sourced?

• The Answer:
	• Simulated: The longitudinal micro-data tracking streams (\(8,400\) total observations across a \(100\)-patient cohort), the first-order temporal autoregressive error tracks [AR(1) with \(\rho = 0.50\)], the stochastic measurement noise (\(\sigma \in \{0.25, 0.75\}\)), and the symptom-driven missingness flags (MAR/MNAR matrix dropouts).
	• Empirically Sourced: The structural baseline framework variables are informed by real-world clinical benchmarks. The baseline center (\(\mu = 120.0\text{ mmHg}\)) is calibrated to standard home systolic blood pressure telemonitoring networks, and the pre-specified safety thresholds (Path 1 limits for hypotension at \(<90\text{ mmHg}\)) match established clinical guidelines for heart failure management.

5. Where are the ground-truth values stored?

• The Answer: To prevent analytical leakage, all ground-truth vectors are kept strictly isolated from the estimator inputs. They are stored inside separate relational tables in the generated outputs catalog:
	• Participant-level ground truth (true hidden baseline intercepts and specific noise allocations): generated/participant_ground_truth.csv
	• Observation-level ground truth (uncorrupted latent physiological trajectories, true safety labels, and exact causes for missing rows): generated/observation_ground_truth.csv

6. Which configurations test failure conditions?

• The Answer: System failure conditions and analytical limits are tested across specific stress cells:
	• Scenario 2 (High-Noise, High-Missingness): Tests estimator resilience against a severe 30% data dropout constraint coupled with a high measurement error standard deviation (\(\sigma = 0.75\)), modeling tracker non-adherence and physical motion artifacts.
	• Scenario 3 (Prolonged-Carryover): Tests structural model vulnerability against a 40% carryover delay lag coefficient, modeling delayed physiological drug clearance during medication transitions.
	• Sensitivity Matrix Grids (GRID_02 & GRID_03): Specifically stress-test the algorithms under non-random, informative missingness paths (MAR phase transitions and MNAR low blood pressure symptom-driven dropouts).

7. How are uncertainty and Monte Carlo error reported?

• The Answer: Uncertainty calibration is reported directly via nominal 95% interval coverage counts (the proportion of simulation runs where the calculated boundaries capture the true effect parameters) across 1,000 independent Monte Carlo replicates per scenario cell. The precision of the simulation engine itself is quantified and reported by calculating the Monte Carlo Standard Error (MCSE) for both bias and interval coverage arrays, following standard statistical methodology reporting protocols.

8. What was independently reproduced?

• The Answer: The entire structural tracking layout has been independently compiled, tested, and verified on an open-source virtual operating system via an automated GitHub Actions Continuous Integration (CI) cloud workflow pipeline. Every script entry—including the data cleaning filters, the json schemas, and the data compilation scripts—passes automated unit testing validations (test_pipeline.py) successfully on every fresh initialization run.

9. What has not been validated?

• The Answer: This study is an advanced pre-clinical methodology framework and statistical stress test; it does not present prospective human clinical efficacy or patient-level safety outcomes. It has not been validated within active clinical hospital workflows, it has not undergone formal integration testing inside real-world EHR environments (FHIR data alignment remains a technical design schema blueprint only), and it does not demonstrate that patient harm was prevented or that a live clinician was contacted in real time.
